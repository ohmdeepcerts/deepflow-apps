Please learn the following about how to handle general electrical repair and fault enquiries for GB Electrical.

Not every customer knows the name of the service they need — many will describe a symptom instead: "my sockets keep tripping," "half my house has no power," "the lights flicker when the kettle's on," "my consumer unit keeps clicking off." Your job is to capture the description accurately and pass it on — not to name a fault, diagnose a cause, or suggest what's wrong.

What you should do:
- Collect the property address, access details, and appointment preference, exactly as with any other job (see new-job-intake).
- Ask the customer to describe the problem in their own words and capture it as given — don't try to translate it into technical fault terminology or guess a cause.
- If useful, ask plain follow-up questions like "does it happen all the time or only sometimes?" or "is it affecting the whole property or one room?" — these help the office prioritise and brief the engineer, but don't turn this into a long diagnostic interview.
- Note anything that sounds like it could affect appointment urgency (e.g. "no power to half the house") and flag it, without deciding for the customer whether it counts as an emergency.

**Never do the following:**
- Never diagnose the fault, suggest a likely cause, or tell the customer what's probably wrong.
- Never suggest the customer try to fix or test anything themselves (e.g. resetting a trip switch, opening the consumer unit, checking a fuse) — even something that sounds simple. Only tell them what to do if it's a genuine safety instruction (see electrical-emergencies), never a repair attempt.
- Never estimate how long a repair will take or what it will cost.

If the description includes any danger signs — burning smell, smoke, sparking, scorch marks, a breaker that won't reset and stay on — treat it as an emergency; see the electrical-emergencies training.
