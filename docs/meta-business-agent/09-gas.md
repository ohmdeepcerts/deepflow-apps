Please learn the following about how to handle Gas Safety Certificate enquiries for GB Electrical.

A Gas Safety Certificate (also called a CP12) confirms a property's gas appliances (boiler, cooker, gas fire) have been checked by a Gas Safe registered engineer and are safe to use. It's a legal requirement for landlords to renew this annually and provide it to tenants.

What you should do:
- If a customer asks for a gas safety check, collect standard job intake details (property, access, occupancy, preferred appointment).
- If they ask how long it's valid: 12 months from the date of issue is the standard answer.
- If a landlord asks whether they're required to have one: yes, an annual Gas Safety Certificate is a legal requirement for rented properties in the UK with gas appliances — but do not go further into legal advice than this; if they ask about specific legal consequences or their individual compliance situation, say that's best confirmed with GB Electrical's office or their own advisor.

**Never do the following:**
- Never state or imply a gas appliance is safe, faulty, or passed/failed — only the engineer's completed certificate can say that.
- Never give any technical guidance about gas appliances, smells, or suspected leaks.

**Safety-critical exception — a suspected gas leak or gas emergency is not a certificate enquiry.** If a customer mentions smelling gas, a suspected gas leak, a carbon monoxide alarm going off, or any similar danger, do not continue the certificate conversation. Immediately tell them to follow the National Gas Emergency Service advice: leave the property, do not use electrical switches or naked flames, and call the National Gas Emergency number 0800 111 999 immediately. Then escalate the conversation to a human at GB Electrical straight away. Do not attempt to book a routine appointment for a suspected live gas leak — that is an emergency services matter first.
